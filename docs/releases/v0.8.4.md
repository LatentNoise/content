The surfaces become one product with four doors instead of four products that
happen to share an engine. Each one offers the others, the sign-in page names
the one you are going back to, signing out actually leaves, and a refusal reads
as a sentence. Underneath, the addresses stopped disagreeing: a deployment now
states one domain and everything else follows. And an instance that has
nowhere else to get them can bundle its own language model and speech service.

**If you self-host, upgrading changes nothing you asked for.** Nothing new is
turned on, nobody is asked to sign in, and the language model and the speech
service stay off until you enable them.

## Four doors, one product

**Each surface offers the others.** Studio, Content Admin and HomeTube each
show the other two as small links in the sidebar, in the same tab. A surface
knows one address, the engine's, and learns its siblings from `/config` —
declared once, as `CONTENT_SURFACES`, rather than configured three times about
each other ([ADR 0038](https://github.com/LatentNoise/content/blob/main/docs/architecture-decisions/0038-the-engine-tells-the-surfaces-about-each-other.md)).

**The sign-in page and the email name where you are going.** "Sign in to
Content Studio", "Sign in to HomeTube" — matched by origin against the declared
surfaces, and never by repeating a URL someone handed the page.

**Signing out leaves.** `GET /auth/sign-out` revokes the session, clears the
cookie in the browser that holds it, and returns to the surface you were on.
The sidebar's Sign out is now that link. The previous button called the engine
from the surface, which revoked the session but left the cookie in the browser
and in the page — a server-rendered surface captures its cookies once, when its
connection opens, and keeps that copy until the page reconnects.

**A refusal is a sentence.** Four places showed a visitor the raw body of an
error — at the exact moment a quota refused them. `APIError.message` gives the
sentence the contract wrote for the person who asked, and every surface now
uses it. A refused visitor also reads which limit they hit and how it resolves
itself (minutes age out, storage needs a deletion, concurrency needs patience),
and sees their three usage counters in the sidebar *before* the refusal.

Where an operator configures it, the refusal can also offer ways forward —
self-hosting first — through `CONTENT_QUOTA_WALL_*`. Empty by default, and the
engine itself never says anything commercial: most installations are one
person's own.

## One address, and it cannot disagree with itself

In production, the public HomeTube's Sign in button pointed at a LAN name
nobody outside the house could reach. Each surface drew its links from its own
`CONTENT_PUBLIC_API_URL`, the engine wrote `CONTENT_PUBLIC_BASE_URL` into its
emails, and nothing compared them.

Two changes close it from both ends:

- **The engine declares where browsers reach it**, as `public_api_url` in
  `/config`, and every link a surface draws — sign in, sign out, documentation,
  downloads — is built on it. The surface's own setting is only the fallback,
  for an engine that declares none.
- **In `token` mode the engine refuses to start on addresses that cannot sign
  anyone in**: no public address, a surface outside the session cookie's
  domain, or an `http` address beside a `Secure` cookie. Each of those used to
  be a sign-in that completed and carried nothing.

### The Helm chart takes one domain

```yaml
domain: example.com
https: true
```

From those two values the chart derives every ingress host
(`api.`, `studio.`, `console.`, `hometube.`), the public URL each UI uses, the
address in sign-in emails, the session cookie on `.example.com`, `Secure` from
`https`, the surfaces, the redirect allowlist and where a sign-in lands. They
were seven settings. A surface can take another subdomain
(`uis.hometube.subdomain`), and any explicit host, URL or config key still
wins.

**Upgrading a chart deployment:** your existing explicit values keep working
unchanged — every derived value yields to one you set. To adopt the domain,
set `domain` and `https`, and remove the hosts, `publicUrl`s, `publicApiUrl`
and the five address keys from `config`. Render before and after with
`helm template` to see that nothing else moves.

## Bundled, for the instance that has nowhere else to get them

**A language model.** Summaries, translations and derived chapters need an
Ollama. `ollama.enabled` deploys one, pulls the configured model and wires
`CONTENT_OLLAMA_URL` by itself; compose has the same behind `--profile llm`.
An address you set always wins. Off by default.

**A speech service.** The published engine image cannot transcribe: it is
Alpine, and the speech-to-text wheels have no musl builds — the
`CONTENT_INSTALL_STT=true` rebuild the documentation used to recommend fails
for everyone, and the documentation now says so. Transcription instead calls a
speech service beside the engine through the OpenAI audio API, and
`speech.enabled` (or `--profile speech`) deploys one that speaks it, for both
speech-to-text and text-to-speech. Audio is only ever sent to a private,
loopback or link-local address.

## Upgrading

No database migration. `/config` gains `surfaces` and `public_api_url`; a
client that does not know them ignores them.

An instance running with `CONTENT_AUTH_MODE=token` now checks its addresses at
startup. If it refuses, the message names each address that cannot work and
why — fix those, or set `domain` in the chart and let it derive them.
